<p>Many women take shilajit as a daily supplement for energy and general well-being. It contains fulvic acid and trace minerals and has a long history in Ayurveda. But most research on shilajit has been done in men or in the laboratory, so for women the honest answer is: it may help some people feel better, and the evidence is still thin.</p>
<p>There are also times when women should not take it at all, most importantly during pregnancy and breastfeeding. This guide covers both sides. For the basics, see <a href="/blogs/guides/what-is-shilajit">What is shilajit?</a></p>
<h2>Why women take shilajit</h2>
<p>Most women who try shilajit are looking for one of a few things: steadier energy through a busy day, support while exercising, or a natural supplement to add to a healthy routine. Ayurveda describes shilajit as a <em>rasayana</em>, taken for vitality at every stage of life, and that tradition is a big part of its appeal.</p>
<h2>Energy and tiredness</h2>
<p>Feeling tired is the most common reason people try shilajit. A review of the research describes it as a traditional "revitaliser" and reports animal and human data on reduced fatigue. Few of those studies included women.</p>
<p>Tiredness has many causes, and some need a doctor. If you are tired most of the time, ask your doctor to check you for iron deficiency, thyroid problems and other common causes before you rely on a supplement.</p>
<h2>Iron and anaemia</h2>
<p>Shilajit contains iron, and you may read that it treats anaemia. There are no good human studies showing that. Iron deficiency anaemia is common in women and is easy to diagnose with a blood test; it is treated with iron prescribed by a doctor, in a measured dose.</p>
<p>Shilajit is not a substitute for that treatment. And if you have a condition that causes too much iron in the body, such as haemochromatosis, speak to your doctor before taking shilajit.</p>
<h2>Exercise and strength</h2>
<p>The best study on shilajit and exercise was done in young men: those who took 500 mg a day of a purified extract for eight weeks kept more of their strength after a tiring test. We do not know whether women would see the same effect. If you train regularly and want to try shilajit, treat it as an extra, not a replacement for good food, rest and training.</p>
<h2>Skin and ageing</h2>
<figure><img src="{{file:xomoashro-journal-benefits-of-shilajit-for-women-skin-health.webp}}" alt="A woman relaxing with a towel on her hair, holding a small dark bottle" width="1024" height="1024"></figure>
<p>Shilajit contains natural antioxidants, which is why it is often linked to skin and healthy ageing. There is no good evidence that taking shilajit reduces wrinkles or blemishes. Sun protection, sleep and a balanced diet remain the proven basics for skin.</p>
<h2>After menopause</h2>
<p>Bone health becomes more important after menopause. Shilajit contains minerals, but there is no reliable evidence that it protects bones in women. Talk to your doctor about bone health, calcium and vitamin D, and about whether a supplement like shilajit is suitable for you, especially if you take other medicines.</p>
<h2>Stress and sleep</h2>
<p>Ayurveda treats shilajit as an adaptogen, a substance that helps the body cope with stress. Laboratory studies support the idea in part, but there are no good human trials on stress, mood or sleep. If stress or poor sleep is affecting your life, speak to a doctor.</p>
<h2>When women should avoid shilajit</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pregnancy:</strong> not recommended. There is no research on its safety in pregnancy. See <a href="/blogs/guides/shilajit-during-pregnancy">Shilajit during pregnancy</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Breastfeeding:</strong> not recommended, for the same reason.</li>
<li><strong>Trying to conceive:</strong> speak to your doctor first.</li>
<li><strong>Too much iron:</strong> for example haemochromatosis.</li>
<li><strong>Hormone treatment or hormone-related conditions:</strong> shilajit has been shown to change hormone levels in men, so ask your doctor.</li>
<li><strong>Regular medication or a long-term condition:</strong> check with your doctor or pharmacist.</li>
</ul>
<h2>How to take it</h2>
<p>Take a pea-sized amount of resin, dissolved in warm water, milk or tea, once a day. Start small and keep to the amount on the label. Our guide <a href="/blogs/guides/how-to-take-shilajit-dosage-tips">How to take shilajit</a> has the details.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Can women take shilajit?</h3>
<p>Yes, most healthy adult women can take it at the amount on the label. Avoid it in pregnancy and while breastfeeding, and speak to your doctor first if you have a health condition or take medication.</p>
<h3>Does shilajit help with anaemia?</h3>
<p>There is no good evidence that it treats anaemia. If you think you might be anaemic, see a doctor for a blood test.</p>
<h3>Can I take shilajit during my period?</h3>
<p>There is no known reason to stop during your period. If you have very heavy periods, speak to your doctor, as they can cause iron deficiency.</p>
<h3>Is shilajit safe while breastfeeding?</h3>
<p>Its safety while breastfeeding has not been studied, so we do not recommend it.</p>
