<p>No. We do not recommend taking shilajit while you are pregnant or breastfeeding, unless your own doctor specifically advises it. There is no research on its safety in pregnancy, it can affect hormones, and its mineral content varies from batch to batch. When a supplement has not been tested in pregnancy, the safe choice is to avoid it.</p>
<p>This is true of our own shilajit too. The rest of this page explains the reasons, what to do if you have already taken some, and better ways to look after your energy while you are expecting.</p>
<h2>Why shilajit is not recommended in pregnancy</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>It has not been studied in pregnancy.</strong> A review of the research found relatively few well-controlled human studies of shilajit at all, and none in pregnant women. We do not know how it affects a developing baby.</li>
<li><strong>It can change hormone levels.</strong> In a trial in men, purified shilajit raised testosterone and DHEAS. Hormones matter a great deal in pregnancy, and any supplement that changes them needs proper study first.</li>
<li><strong>Its make-up varies.</strong> Shilajit is a natural material. The amount of iron and other minerals in it changes from one batch to the next, so you cannot know exactly what you are taking.</li>
<li><strong>Contamination is possible.</strong> Raw or poorly made shilajit can contain heavy metals such as lead. A study of Ayurvedic medicines sold online found that about one in five contained detectable lead, mercury or arsenic. Even small amounts of lead are harmful in pregnancy.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What about its traditional use?</h2>
<p>Shilajit has been used in Ayurveda for centuries as a <em>rasayana</em> for vitality. Long use is not the same as proven safety, especially in pregnancy, when the body and the baby are more sensitive. Many traditional remedies are now known to be unsuitable in pregnancy.</p>
<h2>I have already taken some. What should I do?</h2>
<p>Try not to worry. Stop taking it, and mention it to your doctor or midwife at your next appointment so they have the full picture. If you feel unwell, have pain or bleeding, or are worried, contact your doctor straight away.</p>
<h2>Tiredness in pregnancy</h2>
<p>Feeling tired is very common, especially in the first and last months. Some tiredness is normal, but it can also be a sign of anaemia, which is common in pregnancy and easy to check with a blood test. Your doctor may prescribe iron or folic acid in a measured, safe dose.</p>
<p>Things that help:</p>
<ul>
<li>Rest when you can, and go to bed earlier.</li>
<li>Eat regular meals that include iron-rich foods such as lentils, beans, leafy greens, eggs and meat.</li>
<li>Drink plenty of water.</li>
<li>Gentle activity, such as walking, if your doctor says it is safe.</li>
<li>Take only the supplements your doctor recommends.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Trying to get pregnant</h2>
<p>If you are trying for a baby, speak to your doctor before taking shilajit or any other supplement. Many doctors advise folic acid before conception; ask what is right for you.</p>
<h2>After your baby is born</h2>
<p>Shilajit has not been studied in breastfeeding either, so we recommend waiting until you have stopped breastfeeding. Then speak to your doctor about whether it is suitable for you.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Is shilajit safe during pregnancy?</h3>
<p>Its safety has not been studied, so it is not recommended unless your doctor specifically advises it.</p>
<h3>Can I take shilajit while breastfeeding?</h3>
<p>We do not recommend it. There is no research on its safety while breastfeeding.</p>
<h3>Can shilajit help with tiredness in pregnancy?</h3>
<p>Do not use it for this. Tiredness in pregnancy can be a sign of anaemia, so ask your doctor for a blood test.</p>
<h3>I took shilajit before I knew I was pregnant. Is my baby at risk?</h3>
<p>Stop taking it and tell your doctor or midwife. They can advise you based on your situation.</p>
