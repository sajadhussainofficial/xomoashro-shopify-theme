<p>The short answer: take a pea-sized amount of shilajit resin, dissolve it in warm water, milk or tea, and drink it once a day. Start with less than that for the first few days, and never take more than the amount on your jar's label.</p>
<p>That covers most people. The rest of this guide explains how to measure resin, when to take it, how long to take it for, and when to speak to a doctor first. If you want to know what shilajit is, read <a href="/blogs/guides/what-is-shilajit">What is shilajit?</a> first.</p>
<h2>Step by step</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>Take a pea-sized amount.</strong> Scoop a small portion of resin with a clean, dry spoon or the end of a knife.</li>
<li><strong>Dissolve it.</strong> Stir it into a cup of warm (not boiling) water, milk or tea until it has disappeared. This takes a minute or two.</li>
<li><strong>Drink it once a day.</strong> Many people take it in the morning or early in the day.</li>
</ol>
<p>The taste is earthy and slightly bitter. If you don't like it, milk or a little honey in warm water makes it easier.</p>
<h2>How much to take</h2>
<p>A pea-sized piece of resin is a small amount, and it is the usual daily serving for resin. Keep to the amount on your jar's label, and start with about half of it for the first week to see how you feel.</p>
<p>In published studies, people took measured amounts of a purified shilajit extract, usually between 200 and 500 mg a day. One trial gave 250 mg twice a day for 90 days; another compared 250 mg and 500 mg a day for eight weeks. These studies used standardised extracts, so their numbers are a guide to the range, not an instruction for resin.</p>
<p>More is not better. There is no evidence that larger amounts work better, and they make side effects such as an upset stomach more likely.</p>
<h2>When to take it</h2>
<p>There is no proven best time. Many people prefer the morning because they take it as part of their daily routine and some find it feels energising. If it upsets your stomach, take it after food.</p>
<h2>How long to take it for</h2>
<p>Studies have run for eight to twelve weeks. Give it a few weeks before you decide whether it suits you. If you plan to take it for months at a time, or you take other supplements, mention it to your doctor at your next check-up.</p>
<h2>Resin, powder or capsules?</h2>
<figure><img src="{{file:xomoashro-journal-how-to-take-shilajit-forms.webp}}" alt="Jars of shilajit resin and powder on a wooden table with spices" width="1024" height="1024"></figure>
<p>Shilajit comes in several forms. Resin is the traditional one and the easiest to judge: real purified resin is soft at room temperature, hardens in the cold and dissolves fully in warm water. Powder and drops are easy to mix but easy to dilute. Capsules are convenient, but you cannot see what is inside, and some contain fillers or other ingredients.</p>
<p>Whichever you choose, the amount on the label of that product is the one to follow, because strengths differ between forms and brands. Our article on <a href="/blogs/guides/what-is-shilajit-types-and-benefits">the types of shilajit</a> explains the differences.</p>
<h2>Handling sticky resin</h2>
<ul>
<li>Resin hardens in cold weather and softens in heat. This is normal.</li>
<li>If it is hard, let the jar stand at room temperature, or warm the spoon in hot water first.</li>
<li>If it is very soft, put the jar in a cool place for a while before you scoop.</li>
<li>Always use a clean, dry spoon so no water gets into the jar.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Who should speak to a doctor first</h2>
<p>Shilajit is not suitable for everyone. Speak to your doctor before taking it if you:</p>
<ul>
<li>are pregnant, trying to get pregnant or breastfeeding (we do not recommend shilajit in pregnancy or while breastfeeding; see <a href="/blogs/guides/shilajit-during-pregnancy">our pregnancy guide</a>)</li>
<li>take any regular medication</li>
<li>have a long-term health condition, including heart, kidney or liver disease</li>
<li>have a condition that causes too much iron in the body, such as haemochromatosis</li>
<li>have a hormone-related condition or are on hormone treatment</li>
</ul>
<p>Shilajit is not for children.</p>
<h2>When to stop</h2>
<p>Stop taking it and speak to your doctor if you feel unwell, for example with stomach pain, a rash, dizziness or a racing heartbeat.</p>
<h2>Choosing a good product</h2>
<figure><img src="{{file:xomoashro-journal-how-to-take-shilajit-jars-and-capsules.webp}}" alt="Bottles of shilajit and supplements on a table" width="1024" height="1024"></figure>
<p>The right amount only matters if the product is clean. Choose purified shilajit from a seller who tells you where it was collected and how it was processed, and who can show a lab report for heavy metals. Raw, unprocessed shilajit should never be eaten.</p>
<h2>Storage</h2>
<p>Keep the lid closed and store the jar in a cool, dry place, away from sunlight and out of reach of children.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Can I take shilajit every day?</h3>
<p>Most people take it once a day. Keep to the amount on the label, and talk to your doctor if you plan to take it for a long time.</p>
<h3>Can I take it with other supplements?</h3>
<p>Possibly, but check with your doctor or pharmacist first, especially if you take iron, other minerals or anything hormone-related.</p>
<h3>Can I take it on an empty stomach?</h3>
<p>Yes, if it suits you. If it upsets your stomach, take it after food instead.</p>
<h3>Should I dissolve it in hot or cold water?</h3>
<p>Warm water, milk or tea works best. Resin dissolves slowly in cold liquid.</p>
