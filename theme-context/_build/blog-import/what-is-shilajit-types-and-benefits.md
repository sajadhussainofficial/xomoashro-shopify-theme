<p>Shilajit is a mineral resin that seeps from high mountain rock. You will see it sold in four broad forms: raw shilajit straight from the rock, purified shilajit, a lower grade often called mineral pitch, and shilajit still locked inside its stone. Only purified shilajit is meant to be eaten.</p>
<p>If you are new to shilajit, start with our main guide, <a href="/blogs/guides/what-is-shilajit">What is shilajit?</a> This article goes one step further and explains the types, so you know what you are looking at when you buy.</p>
<h2>Raw shilajit</h2>
<figure><img src="{{file:xomoashro-journal-what-is-shilajit-types-and-benefits-pure-resin.webp}}" alt="Two pieces of raw black shilajit" width="347" height="263"></figure>
<p>Raw shilajit is the material as it is collected: a dark, sticky substance scraped from cracks in the rock, often mixed with sand, stone, plant matter and anything else it touched on the way out. It has a strong, earthy smell.</p>
<p>Raw does not mean better. Unprocessed shilajit can carry soil, fungi and metals from the surrounding rock, so it should not be eaten as it is. "Pure" on a label should mean the shilajit has nothing added to it, not that it has never been cleaned.</p>
<h2>Purified shilajit</h2>
<p>Purified shilajit is raw shilajit that has been cleaned. The usual method is simple: the raw material is dissolved in water, the stones and grit are filtered out, and the liquid is slowly thickened back into a resin. In the traditional Aftabi method the liquid is dried by the sun; in the Agni method it is dried over heat. We explain both in <a href="/blogs/guides/best-shilajit-breakdown-traditional-medicines">Aftabi and Agni shilajit</a>.</p>
<p>This is the form people take. It is usually sold as a soft resin in a jar, and sometimes dried further into powder, drops or capsules. Purification removes visible dirt, but it cannot prove what is inside. That is the job of a lab test for heavy metals and microbes, so look for a seller who can show one.</p>
<h2>Mineral pitch</h2>
<figure><img src="{{file:xomoashro-journal-what-is-shilajit-types-and-benefits-mineral-pitch.webp}}" alt="A tall rock formation in a mountain valley" width="360" height="540"></figure>
<p>"Mineral pitch" is an old English name for shilajit in general, but sellers often use it for lower-grade material: shilajit with more rock and impurities in it, or less careful processing. It is usually cheaper. If a product is far cheaper than others, ask how it was purified and whether it has been tested.</p>
<h2>Rock form</h2>
<figure><img src="{{file:xomoashro-journal-what-is-shilajit-types-and-benefits-rock-form.webp}}" alt="A dark rock outcrop lit from behind" width="360" height="540"></figure>
<p>Shilajit is sometimes sold still inside the stone it formed in, as a curiosity or for people who want to see where it comes from. Rock-form shilajit is not ready to eat. It has to be extracted and purified first, which takes time and care.</p>
<h2>Types or forms? Resin, powder and capsules</h2>
<p>People often mix up the type of shilajit with the form it is sold in. Purified shilajit can be sold as:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Resin:</strong> the traditional form. Soft and sticky at room temperature, it dissolves in warm water. It is the easiest form to check by eye, smell and texture.</li>
<li><strong>Powder:</strong> dried resin, ground down. Easy to mix but easy to dilute with fillers.</li>
<li><strong>Capsules or tablets:</strong> convenient and measured, but you cannot see what is inside, and some contain other ingredients.</li>
<li><strong>Drops:</strong> shilajit dissolved in liquid. Strength varies a lot between brands.</li>
</ul>
<p>Whatever the form, the same questions apply: where it was collected, how it was purified, and whether a lab has tested it.</p>
<h2>What the research says about its benefits</h2>
<p>Shilajit has a long history in Ayurveda, where it is used as a <em>rasayana</em>, a substance taken for vitality. Modern research is more limited than most marketing suggests. A review in <em>Phytotherapy Research</em> found that relatively few well-controlled human studies exist, although laboratory and animal studies point to antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity and a possible role in reducing fatigue.</p>
<p>The human studies that do exist are small. One placebo-controlled trial in 63 active young men found that 500 mg a day of a purified extract for eight weeks helped them keep more of their strength after a tiring exercise test; a lower dose made no difference. That is interesting, but it is one study, in one group of people.</p>
<p>So treat the benefits as promising, not proven. Shilajit is a food supplement, not a medicine, and it is not a treatment for any illness.</p>
<h2>Which type should you choose?</h2>
<ul>
<li>Choose <strong>purified</strong> shilajit, never raw.</li>
<li>Prefer a seller who tells you <strong>where</strong> it was collected and <strong>how</strong> it was purified.</li>
<li>Ask for a <strong>lab report</strong> that covers heavy metals.</li>
<li>Be wary of very low prices and big health promises.</li>
</ul>
<p>For how much to take and how to take it, see <a href="/blogs/guides/how-to-take-shilajit-dosage-tips">How to take shilajit</a>.</p>
<h2>Frequently asked questions</h2>
<h3>Which type of shilajit is the best?</h3>
<p>Purified shilajit resin from a known source, with a lab report, is the safest choice. "Best" is less about the name on the jar and more about cleaning and testing.</p>
<h3>Is raw shilajit stronger?</h3>
<p>No. Raw shilajit is not more effective, and it can contain dirt, microbes and metals. Do not eat it unprocessed.</p>
<h3>Is mineral pitch the same as shilajit?</h3>
<p>Historically the names meant the same thing. Today "mineral pitch" is often used for lower-grade material, so check how it was processed and tested.</p>
<h3>Is shilajit resin better than capsules?</h3>
<p>Neither form is automatically better. Resin is easier to judge by look, smell and how it dissolves; capsules are easier to measure. The source and the testing matter more than the form.</p>
